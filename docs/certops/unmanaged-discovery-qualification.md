# Unmanaged discovery qualification

`unmanaged-discovery-api` invokes the production filesystem collector against an actual temporary public certificate file, then sends its observations through a registered protocol agent to the real API and PostgreSQL database. OpenSSL generates the certificate; its public fingerprint is independently calculated before collection. The private key lives outside the scanned directory, and the fixture never advertises Windows CNG or machine-store custody.

Seven mandatory cases cover source setup, blocked management admission, fresh repeated observation, malformed scan preservation, public inventory visibility, restored admission and cleanup. The fixture explicitly sets its isolated workspace quota to zero. Actual PostgreSQL admission fails while the authenticated observation remains retained: one certificate identity and one fresh slot are visible, with no managed certificate or open management period. A repeated real scan advances the capture time without duplicating the identity or slot. The public unmanaged filter and detail show a confirmed present stored copy; this does not imply a served TLS endpoint.

Replacing the owned public file with malformed bytes exercises the real parser. The collector must produce no observation, send no fabricated evidence, and leave the retained identity and its previous genuine capture unchanged. Restoring the public bytes and raising the fixture quota to one lets an actual rescan admit exactly one managed certificate and management period under the same identity. The unmanaged filter then excludes it.

The quota is a declared database prerequisite. This suite does not qualify a SaaS billing-plan transition, Windows store discovery, an installed service, renewal eligibility or issuer execution. The evidence transport uses actual authenticated HTTP; its small fixture adapter assigns required evidence identifiers and does not fabricate responses. Reports omit private bytes and raw subprocess output. Cleanup independently removes the fixture workspace and the checked run-owned temporary directory.

All seven cases passed in focused local execution on Windows and in an isolated pinned Debian 12 container. Both source-bound reports passed independent strict verification. Hosted execution remains pending; local reports do not qualify the final committed release artifact.
